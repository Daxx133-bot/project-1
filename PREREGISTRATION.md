## 0. Why this exists

A research-integrity audit (`_audit/AUDIT_REPORT.md`, 2026-09-23) found that the prior
results were produced by (a) a dataset in which 48 of 107 rows were generated by
`random.uniform()` with magnitudes keyed to each service's own centrality rank, (b)
weights grid-searched against the same outcome they were then validated against, and
(c) an outcome variable that measured container restart rather than application recovery.
The headline ρ = 0.897 was a maximum over a search on 49 %-synthetic data; on the
cleanest real dataset the same quantity is **ρ = 0.0605, p = 0.85**.

This preregistration exists so that the next result cannot be produced that way.

---

## 1. Hypotheses

**H1 (primary).** The fan-out corrected hybrid criticality metric ranks services by
recovery time better than in-degree centrality does.

**H2 (secondary).** Classical composite centrality ranking systematically demotes
high-fan-out orchestrator services relative to their measured impact — the
"centrality-induced false negative".

**H0 for both:** no monotone association beyond chance. **H0 is a publishable outcome.**

---

## 2. The hybrid metric — exact formula

For each service $v$ in the directed service dependency graph $G$:

$$
\mathrm{Criticality}(v) \;=\; w_{in}\,C_{in}(v) \;+\; w_{out}\,C_{out}(v)\cdot\phi(v) \;+\; w_{btw}\,C_{btw}(v)
$$

$$
\phi(v) \;=\; \frac{d^{+}(v)}{\max_{u \in V} d^{+}(u)}
$$

where:

| Term | Definition | NetworkX call |
|---|---|---|
| $C_{in}(v)$ | in-degree centrality, $d^-(v)/(n-1)$ | `nx.in_degree_centrality(G)` |
| $C_{out}(v)$ | out-degree centrality, $d^+(v)/(n-1)$ | `nx.out_degree_centrality(G)` |
| $C_{btw}(v)$ | betweenness, **directed** normalisation $1/((n-1)(n-2))$ | `nx.betweenness_centrality(G, normalized=True)` |
| $\phi(v)$ | fan-out weight, **linear** in out-degree | — |

**Three corrections to the current manuscript, which must be carried into the rewrite:**

1. $\phi(v)$ is **linear**, $d^+/\max d^+$. The manuscript's §3.4 also states a
   *quadratic* form $[d^+]^2/((n-1)\max d^+)$. The code has only ever implemented the
   linear form. The two coincide numerically **only for the maximum-out-degree node**,
   which is why the discrepancy went unnoticed. The linear form is what is preregistered.
2. Degree centrality on a DiGraph sums in- and out-edges over $(n-1)$, **not** $2(n-1)$.
3. Eigenvector centrality is **L2**-normalised and is **degenerate on both graphs** —
   it is ≈0 for 10 of 12 Social Network services and 6 of 7 Hotel Reservation services,
   with the mass sitting on a 2-cycle. It is reported as a baseline **with that
   degeneracy stated**, not as a fair competitor.

Implementation: `centrality/hybrid_weight_optimizer.py::compute_hybrid`, verified against
the formula in `tests/test_structural_metrics.py::test_hybrid_matches_the_documented_formula`.

## 3. Weight protocol

**Preregistered choice: `cross-arch`.**

- Tune $(w_{in}, w_{out}, w_{btw})$ on **Social Network only**, grid step 0.05,
  $w_{in}+w_{out}+w_{btw}=1$, all non-negative (231 triplets).
- Freeze the winner to `centrality/output/tuned_weights.lock.json`.
- Evaluate **once** on **Hotel Reservation**. That single held-out ρ is the only
  reportable weight-dependent number.
- The tuning-set ρ is **not a result** and will be reported only as "maximum over 231
  triplets on the tuning architecture".
- Enforced in code: `--protocol` is required, re-tuning over an existing lockfile is
  refused, and a second evaluation is refused.

`--protocol fixed` (weights from `centrality/metric_constants.py`, outcome data never
read) is implemented as the fallback and will be used if open question 1 resolves that way.

>

## 4. Primary outcome variable

**$T_{rec}$ — application recovery time**, in seconds, defined operationally as:

> The elapsed time from the instant of fault injection until Δp95, Δp99, Δerror-rate and
> downstream-affected-count have **simultaneously** returned to within **10 %** of their
> pre-fault baseline, and have **held** there for a **6-second confirmation period**.
> Measured by polling Jaeger every **3 seconds** over a **10-second** trailing window,
> up to a maximum of **180 seconds**.

- Tolerance is **one-sided**: faster or less-errored than baseline counts as recovered.
- A polling window **never reaches back before the fault instant**.
- An empty trace list is **"no signal"**, never "zero latency, therefore recovered".
- If baseline is exactly zero, recovery requires the current value to be zero — no
  threshold is invented.

**Censoring.** If recovery is not observed within 180 s, the observation is **censored**:
`recovery_time_s = None`, `recovery_censored = True`, and `recovery_observed_until_s`
records the follow-up time. **A censored observation is never filled with a number**, and
never averaged in as if it were a duration. This is the exact defect that produced the
manuscript's 61.07 s figure (the old 60 s cap returned on timeout).

Implementation: `measurement/recovery_probe.py::probe_recovery`, 11 tests in
`tests/test_recovery_probe.py`.

**Secondary outcomes** (reported, not primary): Δp95, Δp99, Δerror-rate,
downstream-affected-count, and `container_restart_time_s` — the old measurement, kept
under its honest name as a distinct infrastructure metric.

> ### ⚠ OPEN QUESTION 2 — needs your decision before sign-off
>
> Under the corrected definition, **latency and CPU faults may recover within one poll
> interval**, because these faults do not stop the container and the load generator keeps
> the pipeline warm. If so, $T_{rec}$ for those fault types will cluster at ~3 s and
> carry almost no rank information — the same degeneracy as the old metric, for a
> different reason.
>
> Options: **(a)** report $T_{rec}$ for kill faults only and treat latency/CPU with the
> Δp95 outcome instead; **(b)** shorten the poll interval to 1 s to resolve fast
> recoveries; **(c)** run the pilot first and decide from it — but then the decision is
> data-dependent and must be disclosed as such.
>
> My recommendation: **(b) now, then (a) if the pilot still shows clustering** — and
> disclose that (a) was a pilot-informed decision if it is taken.

## 5. Baseline metrics for comparison

All ten are computed for every service and compared against every outcome. No metric may
be dropped after seeing results.

| # | Metric | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Degree | `nx.degree_centrality` |
| 2 | In-degree | `nx.in_degree_centrality` |
| 3 | Out-degree | `nx.out_degree_centrality` |
| 4 | Betweenness | `nx.betweenness_centrality(normalized=True)`, directed |
| 5 | Closeness | `nx.closeness_centrality` |
| 6 | Eigenvector | `nx.eigenvector_centrality`, L2 — **degenerate, disclosed** |
| 7 | PageRank | `nx.pagerank(alpha=0.85)` |
| 8 | Composite | mean of the 7 normalised ranks, ties `method="min"` |
| 9 | **Descendant count** | `descendant_count()` — size of the reachable set |
| 10 | **Dominator subtree size** | `dominator_subtree_size()` — nodes that become unreachable |
| — | **Hybrid (under test)** | §2 |

Metrics 9 and 10 are new, purely structural, and use no outcome data. Metric 10 is the
stronger operational claim: it counts services actually disconnected by a failure, rather
than everything downstream regardless of alternative routes.

## 6. Statistical plan

**Primary test.** Spearman rank correlation $\rho$ between each metric and $T_{rec}$,
computed **per architecture**, at the **service level** ($n = 12$ SN, $n = 7$ HR), with
per-service values aggregated across repetitions by the **median** (robust to the
long-tailed recovery distribution).

**Censoring.** Because $T_{rec}$ is censored, the confirmatory test is **survival-based**,
not correlation-based:

- Kaplan–Meier estimates of the recovery-time distribution, stratified by metric tercile.
- **Log-rank test** for a difference between terciles.
- **Cox proportional-hazards** model with the metric as a continuous covariate, reporting
  the hazard ratio with a 95 % CI. This is the primary inferential result.
- Spearman ρ is reported alongside, computed on uncensored observations only, and
  **explicitly labelled as biased toward fast recoveries**.

If fewer than 10 % of observations are censored, the Cox model is reported but Spearman ρ
is promoted to primary. That threshold is fixed here, in advance.

**Multiple comparisons.** The confirmatory family is **11 metrics × 1 primary outcome ×
2 architectures = 22 tests**. Correction: **Benjamini–Hochberg FDR at q = 0.05**, applied
across the whole family of 22. Secondary outcomes (§4) form a separate, exploratory
family, corrected separately and labelled exploratory.

**Effect size.** $\rho$ with bootstrap 95 % CI (10 000 resamples, BCa) for correlations;
hazard ratio with 95 % CI for Cox. **A significant result with a CI crossing a
negligible effect is reported as inconclusive, not as support.**

**Power.** With $n = 12$ services, detecting $\rho = 0.7$ at $\alpha = 0.05$ has roughly
70 % power; $n = 7$ has under 40 %. **This study is underpowered at the service level and
will say so plainly.** Increasing repetitions does not fix this — the unit of analysis is
the service, and there are only 12 and 7 of them. This is a structural limitation of
two-benchmark designs and will appear in the Limitations section, not be papered over.

## 7. Data collection plan

**Target: 5 repetitions per (service × fault type), balanced, for both architectures.**

| Architecture | Services | Fault types | Reps | Runs |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Social Network | 12 | 3 | 5 | **180** |
| Hotel Reservation | 8 (incl. `user`) | 3 | 5 | **120** |
| | | | | **300 total** |

- **Balance is mandatory.** The existing batches are unbalanced precisely on the services
  carrying the headline claims (`compose-post-service` 8/8/7 vs 3 elsewhere;
  `reservation` 9 vs 3). Any run that fails is re-run, not topped up unevenly.
- Faults: Pumba `kill --signal SIGKILL`; `netem delay --time 100 --jitter 100`;
  `stress --cpu 2`.
- Protocol per run: 60 s warm-up → 30 s baseline → 30 s fault (60 s for kill) → recovery
  probe to 180 s → 30 s cooldown. **This is what will actually be run**; the current
  manuscript states 60 s warm-up while the code used 15–30 s.
- **A run with `telemetry_ok = False` (zero baseline traces) is discarded and re-run.**
  It is never analysed as a zero.

**Preconditions — none of this may start until all are met:**

1. Locust installed and the workload verified to generate traffic.
2. `_locustfile_hotel.py` fixed so `/user` and `/reservation` stop returning HTTP 400, and
   the HR graph re-extracted showing the `user` node and its 2 edges (8 nodes / 8 edges).
3. `trace_count` and `telemetry_ok` written for every run.
4. Social Network traces re-captured and `sdg/output/sdg.json` regenerated — the traces
   behind the current graph were overwritten and no longer exist.
5. A pilot of 1 repetition per (service × fault) passes with non-zero telemetry.

## 8. Reporting commitments

1. **If the result is null, it will be reported as null.** Specifically: if the hybrid
   metric does not beat in-degree on the held-out architecture after FDR correction, the
   paper will say the fan-out correction did not predict recovery time under this
   measurement setup. No metric will be redefined, no fault type dropped, no service
   excluded, and no outcome swapped to improve it.
2. **Every number will be produced by a script reading `data/raw/`**, with the script and
   commit recorded. No number will be typed into the manuscript by hand.
3. **All 22 confirmatory tests will be reported**, significant or not, with corrected
   p-values — no selective reporting of the favourable cells.
4. **Deviations from this document will be listed** in a "Deviations from
   preregistration" section, with dates and reasons.
5. **The audit will be cited.** The paper will state that an earlier analysis was
   withdrawn after fabricated data was found, and what changed. Quietly publishing
   corrected numbers without saying why is not acceptable.
6. **The structural result stands on its own.** That classical composite ranking demotes
   high-fan-out orchestrators (compose-post 7 → 1; frontend 7 → 1) is reproducible from
   `centrality/output/` alone and requires no fault-injection data. If H1 is null, this
   remains a real, defensible contribution and becomes the paper's main claim.
